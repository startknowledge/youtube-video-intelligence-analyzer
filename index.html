About Level 1 — Basic Video Intelligence

What this section shows

Level 1 presents the fundamental public facts about the video: its title, channel, publishing date, duration, view count, likes, comments, category, language, definition, dimensions and caption availability. These are the same signals YouTube search uses to understand what a video is about and who it is for.

Why it matters for SEO

View count, likes and comment count contribute to YouTube’s engagement signal. Duration affects watch-time potential. Caption availability improves accessibility and helps YouTube’s automatic speech recognition index the content. Category and language help YouTube match the video to the right audience.

What to check

  • Is the video published recently or is it an evergreen evergreen?
  • Is duration aligned with the format (Shorts under 60s, long-form 8–20 min)?
  • Are captions available in the target language?
  • Does the like-to-view ratio suggest strong audience satisfaction?

📊 How the SEO Score is calculated

The SEO Score is a weighted composite metric that summarizes how well a YouTube video is optimized for search and discovery. It ranges from 0 to 100 and combines four sub-scores: Title Optimization, Description Quality, Keyword Coverage and Content Structure.

Scoring bands

  • 80–100: Excellent optimization. Focus on distribution and thumbnail CTR.
  • 60–79: Good foundation. Improve keyword placement and description depth.
  • 40–59: Needs work. Rework title, expand description, add chapters.
  • Below 40: High opportunity. Follow the suggested titles and content gaps.

How to improve it

  • Move the primary keyword into the first 40 characters of the title.
  • Write a description of 250+ words including the primary keyword in the first 150 characters.
  • Add chapter markers to make the content easier to scan.
  • Use 8–15 relevant tags, mixing broad and long-tail keywords.
  • Add a clear call to action and a link to a related video or playlist.

Why it matters

A higher SEO score correlates with better visibility in YouTube search, suggested videos and Google video results. It is not a guarantee of ranking — but it removes the most common optimization barriers that prevent videos from reaching their potential.

🎯 How the Title Score is calculated

The Title Score evaluates the YouTube title for search optimization and click-through potential. It is based on length, keyword placement, clarity, emotional or curiosity triggers and the absence of clickbait patterns.

What a strong title looks like

  • Length: 40–70 characters (visible on mobile without truncation).
  • Keyword position: Primary keyword appears within the first 40 characters.
  • Clarity: The viewer instantly understands what the video delivers.
  • Specificity: Numbers, years, or concrete outcomes outperform vague phrasing.
  • Emotion: Curiosity, benefit or urgency — used honestly.

Common mistakes

  • ALL CAPS in the title.
  • Misleading clickbait that hurts watch time and trust.
  • Keyword stuffing (multiple keywords back-to-back).
  • Too short (below 30 characters) or too long (over 70 characters).

How to improve it

Use the Better Titles section of the report for AI-generated alternatives. Test two versions in two uploads to compare CTR over time.

📝 How the Description Score is calculated

The Description Score measures how well the YouTube description supports viewers and search engines. YouTube uses the first 150 characters as the snippet shown in search and suggested results — so the opening line matters most.

Best practices

  • First 150 characters include the primary keyword and a clear summary.
  • Total length between 250 and 5000 characters.
  • Add timestamps or chapter markers.
  • Include 3–5 relevant hashtags (YouTube shows the first three).
  • Add links to related videos, playlists or external resources.
  • Include a subscribe line and a call to action.

What lowers the score

  • Empty or one-line descriptions.
  • Copy-pasted boilerplate that adds no context.
  • Excessive hashtags (over 15 hurts more than helps).
  • Links that are broken or lead to unrelated pages.

🧠 How the Content Score is calculated

The Content Score reflects how well the video’s public signals match real search intent. It analyzes topic clarity, keyword variety, chapter structure, tag relevance and the alignment between title, description and the video’s apparent subject.

Signals used

  • Primary keyword appears in title, description and tags.
  • Long-tail variations distributed naturally.
  • Chapters or timestamps included (where format allows).
  • Content type clearly identifiable (tutorial, review, vlog, explainer, etc.).
  • Search intent (informational, navigational, transactional) is well matched.

How to raise the Content Score

  • Add the primary keyword in the first 150 characters of the description.
  • Use the keyword suggestions to write additional videos on adjacent topics.
  • Add chapters for any video over 5 minutes.
  • Rename tags to closely match what viewers search for.
  • Repurpose your content across Shorts, blog posts and FAQs to reinforce the topic cluster.

4 Keyword Intelligence
About Keyword Intelligence

Keywords are grouped into three layers. Primary keywords are the main topics the video targets. Secondary keywords are supporting terms that expand topical relevance. Long-tail keywords are specific phrases that attract focused, high-intent viewers. Suggested hashtags are ready to copy into your description.

How to use these keywords

  • Add 1 primary keyword to the title (near the front).
  • Distribute 3–5 secondary keywords naturally in the description.
  • Use long-tail keywords as chapter names or as ideas for new videos.
  • Copy hashtags into the first line of the description.

Why keyword layering works

YouTube’s algorithm rewards topical depth. A video that covers a topic with a clear primary term, supported by secondary and long-tail variations, tends to rank for many related queries at once — instead of a single search term.

5 YouTube Tags
About YouTube Tags

Tags are less important than title and description, but they still help YouTube and Google understand the video’s topic and detect misspellings. Aim for 8–15 tags that mix:

  • The exact primary keyword
  • 2–3 variants of the primary keyword
  • 3–5 secondary keywords
  • 2–3 long-tail phrases
  • Common misspellings (only if they are widely used)

Avoid tag stuffing with hundreds of tags — it dilutes relevance instead of improving it.

—
6 Chapters
About Chapters

Chapters are timestamped sections inside the description. They improve user experience, boost watch time, and give YouTube extra context for indexing. YouTube requires:

  • The first chapter must start at 00:00
  • Minimum 3 chapters
  • Each chapter should be at least 10 seconds long

Use chapter names that contain long-tail keywords from the Keyword Intelligence section — this is one of the easiest ways to increase topical coverage.

About AI Content Intelligence

The AI Content Intelligence section summarizes what the video is about and how it is likely perceived by viewers. It is generated from the public metadata using AI-assisted analysis.

What is included

  • Main Topic — what the video is fundamentally about.
  • Content Type — tutorial, review, explainer, vlog, listicle, etc.
  • Difficulty — beginner, intermediate, advanced.
  • Search Intent — informational, navigational, commercial, transactional.
  • Summary — a short paragraph capturing the video’s core message.
  • Key Points — the main takeaways a viewer would expect.

How to use it

Use the summary and key points to write your description and chapters. If the AI summary surprises you — for example, if it captures a different main topic than you intended — that is a signal that your public metadata may need clarification.

      About Pro Intelligence

      Pro Intelligence is the idea generation engine. It takes the video’s topic and generates content ideas across formats — Shorts, blogs, related videos and FAQs. These ideas help you build a topic cluster around the video and rank for more queries.

      How to use each list

      • Shorts Ideas — short vertical clips you can cut from the video or shoot fresh.
      • Blog Ideas — long-form article topics that expand the video into written content.
      • Related Ideas — follow-up videos your audience will likely watch next.
      • FAQ Ideas — questions to answer in the description, in a follow-up video, or on your website with FAQ schema.

      Content clustering strategy

      Publish 1 primary video + 3–5 Shorts + 2 blog posts + 1 FAQ page around the same topic. Interlink them and reference them in each other’s descriptions. This is the single most effective way to grow a channel organically.

              10 Better Titles
              About Better Title Suggestions

              These titles are generated as alternatives to the current one. They follow best practices: keyword near the front, 40–70 characters, clear benefit, and no clickbait.

              How to test them

              • Pick 1–2 titles and use them in future uploads.
              • Compare click-through rate over 7 days.
              • Keep the winner and re-test the second choice in the next upload.

              Changing an existing video’s title is fine — YouTube updates the title in search and suggestions within a few hours. Do not change the title more than once every 7 days, since frequent changes can temporarily disrupt ranking signals.

              11 Content Gap Opportunities
              About Content Gap Opportunities

              Content gaps are topics that competitors cover but your video does not. Filling these gaps is one of the fastest ways to grow reach because you are answering questions viewers already have.

              How to use gaps

              • Turn each gap into a Short, a blog post or a follow-up video.
              • Add gap topics as new chapters to an existing video.
              • Include gap keywords in the video description and tags.

              13 Related YouTube Videos
              About Related Videos

              Related videos are fetched from YouTube’s public data for the video’s topic. Use them to benchmark competitors, spot patterns in top-ranking titles, and discover keyword variations you may have missed.

              What to look for

              • Which titles rank highest — what format do they use?
              • Which thumbnails get the most views?
              • Which topics recur across top videos?
              • What gaps exist that none of them cover?

              12.5 Video Transcript
              AUTO
              About Video Transcript

              The transcript is the auto-generated or manually-uploaded text version of the video's audio. YouTube generates captions automatically for most videos in English and many other languages. When a manual transcript is uploaded by the creator, YouTube uses that instead — usually with higher accuracy.

              Why the transcript matters

              • SEO: Search engines read the transcript text as page content.
              • Accessibility: Deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers can follow along.
              • Content repurposing: Use the transcript to write blogs, FAQs, and Shorts scripts.
              • Translation: Most transcripts can be translated to other languages.

              How to use it in this report

              • Skim the transcript to spot the main talking points.
              • Copy key phrases to use as long-tail keywords.
              • Turn important passages into Shorts clips or blog sections.
              • Download the .txt version for offline use.

              Availability

              Not every video has a transcript. Private, unlisted, or very new videos may not have captions yet. When a transcript is unavailable, this section clearly states that — the analyzer does not generate fake text.

              📖 How to Use YouTube Video Intelligence Analyzer — Complete Guide (click to expand)

              How to Use YouTube Video Intelligence Analyzer

              YouTube Video Intelligence Analyzer is a free online tool built for content creators, video marketers, SEO professionals, agencies and bloggers who want to understand how well a YouTube video is optimized and where the biggest growth opportunities are. This guide walks through every part of the tool so you can get the maximum value in the shortest time.

              Step 1 — Copy a public YouTube video URL

              Open YouTube in any browser or the YouTube app, find the video you want to analyze, and copy its URL from the address bar. You can also use the Share button and choose "Copy link". The analyzer works with regular videos, YouTube Shorts, live replays and premieres — as long as the video is publicly accessible.

              Step 2 — Paste the URL into the analyzer

              Return to this page, paste the URL into the input box under the hero section, and click Analyze Video. The tool fetches the public metadata (title, description, tags, chapters, category, language, definition, dimensions and captions) and prepares a full report.

              Step 3 — Review the score grid

              At the top of the report you will see four scores: SEO Score, Title Score, Description Score and Content Score. Each score is out of 100. Click the ⓘ icon next to any score to read a detailed explanation of how the score is calculated and what specific improvements will raise it.

              Step 4 — Explore the report sections

              The report is divided into logical sections that mirror how YouTube search and recommendation systems evaluate a video:

              • Basic Video Information — Video ID, category, language, definition, dimensions and caption availability.
              • Description Analysis — Character count, word count, hashtag count, link count and readability.
              • SEO Intelligence — Title optimization, description quality, keyword coverage and content structure.
              • Keyword Intelligence — Primary, secondary and long-tail keywords plus suggested hashtags.
              • YouTube Tags — Tags currently attached to the video.
              • Chapters — Chapter markers found in the description.
              • AI Content Intelligence — Main topic, content type, difficulty, search intent, summary and key points.
              • Audience & Tone — Likely audience profile, tone signals and sentiment.
              • Pro Intelligence — Shorts ideas, blog ideas, related ideas and FAQ ideas.
              • Better Titles — Click-optimized title suggestions.
              • Content Gap Opportunities — Topics your video did not cover but competitors often do.
              • Related YouTube Videos — Public related videos for research and benchmarking.
              • Location Intelligence — Public geographic signals (when available) plotted on a world map.

              Step 5 — Export or print the report

              Use the Export JSON button in the sidebar to download the full analysis as a JSON file for your records. Use Print Report to open a print-friendly view of the report which you can save as PDF for client delivery.

              Understanding each score

              SEO Score

              The SEO Score is a combined metric that reflects overall optimization quality. It is calculated from the title score, description score, keyword coverage and content structure. A score above 80 indicates strong optimization; 60–79 needs refinement; below 60 signals significant opportunity.

              Title Score

              The Title Score evaluates the YouTube title for length, keyword placement, click-through potential and clarity. Ideal titles fall between 40–70 characters, include the primary keyword near the front, avoid clickbait, and use emotional or curiosity triggers naturally.

              Description Score

              The Description Score reviews how well the description is written for both viewers and search engines. Factors include character count (ideal 250–5000), keyword placement in the first 150 characters, presence of links, hashtags, chapters and clear calls to action.

              Content Score

              The Content Score gauges whether the video's public content signals (topics, structure, keywords, chapters, tags) match the search intent. Higher scores mean your content is well-aligned with what viewers search for and what YouTube's algorithm rewards.

              Best practices

              • Analyze your top 5 and bottom 5 videos to spot patterns.
              • Compare your videos with 2–3 competitors to find keyword gaps.
              • Use Shorts ideas and blog ideas to repurpose content across formats.
              • Re-run the analysis after each edit — scores update in real time.
              • Use keyword suggestions as seeds for future titles and chapters.

The analyzer is designed to be helpful for beginners who want clear direction and for advanced users who want structured data to make faster decisions.
